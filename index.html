Look for elevating leg rests. With an ankle in a cast, the leg should stay up. Many basic "transport" chairs (the kind someone else pushes) don't have leg rests, so ask for them.

Care Tips for a Broken Ankle (Three Fractures)

  • Keep the leg up. Raise it above heart level as much as you can, especially in the first few days, to bring the swelling down.
  • Ice it. 15 to 20 minutes at a time, always with a cloth between the ice and the skin or cast. Keep the cast dry while you do.
  • No weight on it, even in the cast, until the doctor says so.
  • Pillow under the calf, not the heel. Resting the heel directly on a pillow can cause pressure sores.
  • Keep the cast clean and dry. Use a cast cover or plastic bag for bathing, and never poke anything inside it to scratch.
  • Ask the doctor about clots. Sitting still for weeks raises the risk, so ask whether he needs a blood thinner or compression stockings.
  • Line up help for daily tasks. Since he can't walk, even in Crocs, plan for help with groceries, meals, laundry, getting to appointments, and getting safely to the bathroom. Clear the floors of rugs and clutter.
Call the doctor right away if he has numbness or tingling, toes that turn blue, pale or cold, pain that gets severe or won't ease, a fever, or a cast that feels too tight. Sudden chest pain or trouble breathing means calling 911.

Good to know

  • Where a shop doesn't post its rates, call them. Prices here are only what each website showed.
  • Buying new: a basic Medline transport chair on Amazon is about $142.
  • Insurance: Medicare Part B covers a wheelchair when a doctor says it's needed, and you'd pay about 20% after the deductible. Ask the orthopedist's office for a prescription. Medicare details
  • Knee scooter: some people in a cast find one easier than a wheelchair. Ask the doctor first. NYC Mobility rents them from $50 a day, and Homepro from $75 a week.
